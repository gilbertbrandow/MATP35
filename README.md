# MATP35 - Linear Functional Analysis

MATP35 Linear Functional Analysis, 7.5 credits, is an elective second-cycle course for a Master of Science degree in mathematics. The course presents the principles of functional analysis and their applications, including the basic properties of Banach and Hilbert spaces and the spectral theory of bounded and compact linear operators. The language of instruction is English.

The course is given in parallel with MATP45 Specialised Course in Linear Functional Analysis, where applications and examples are studied in greater depth. Students are strongly recommended to take both courses.

## Course content

The course covers:

- Normed spaces, Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces, and their duals
- The Hahn-Banach Theorem
- Reflexive spaces, weak and weak-star convergence and topologies, and the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem
- Hilbert-space geometry, including orthogonality, closed subspaces, the projection theorem, and the Riesz Representation Theorem
- Orthonormal sets, Bessel's inequality, complete orthonormal sets, and Parseval's identity
- The Baire Category Theorem and its consequences: the uniform boundedness, open mapping, inverse mapping, and closed graph theorems
- Bounded and compact linear operators on Banach spaces and their spectra
- The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces

## Learning outcomes

After completing the course, the student should be able to:

- Give a detailed account of the concepts, theorems, and methods treated in the course
- Identify the main theorems, explain the principal ideas behind them, and carry out the steps in their proofs
- Give non-trivial examples in which the theorems apply
- Integrate knowledge from different parts of the course when solving problems
- Identify problems suited to methods from functional analysis and apply appropriate solution methods
- Explain mathematical solutions coherently in speech and writing using appropriate terminology
- Identify applications of functional analysis in areas such as differential equations, function spaces, and operator theory

## Assessment

The examination consists of:

- A written examination worth 5 credits
- An oral examination worth 2.5 credits
- The oral examination may only be taken after passing the written examination
- A resit written examination is offered shortly after the regular written examination

Both the written and oral examinations must be passed. The current material does not specify the duration or permitted aids for the 2026 written examination, or the date and detailed format of the oral examination.

## Grading

The grading scale for the complete course is Fail, Pass, and Pass with distinction. The written and oral subcourses are each graded Fail or Pass.

Pass with distinction requires at least 75% of the total maximum number of points from the written and oral examinations. In this calculation, the maximum points available from the written and oral examinations are weighted 3:1.

## Entry requirements

Admission requires:

- English 6
- At least 90 credits in pure mathematics
- Knowledge corresponding to a 7.5-credit course in Integration Theory, including the Lebesgue integral

The course may not be included in a degree together with MATP15 Linear Functional Analysis, 7.5 credits.
